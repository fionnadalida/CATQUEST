Cornish Rex Cat

History & Origin

The Cornish Rex originated in Bodmin Moor, Cornwall, England, in the mid-20th century. The breed developed from a spontaneous natural genetic mutation that produced an unusually fine, curly coat.

On July 21, 1950, a tortoiseshell-and-white female cat named Serena gave birth to five kittens on a farm owned by Nina Ennismore and Winifred Macalister. Four kittens had normal short coats, while one cream-colored male kitten was born with a fine, curled and wavy coat. He was named Kallibunker, commonly called Kalli, and became the foundation of the Cornish Rex breed.

Kallibunker developed a distinctive body shape, with a slender body, long legs, narrow head, large ears and a long, tapering tail. His unusual coat was caused by a recessive mutation, meaning that the characteristic could be carried by cats without always being visibly expressed.

Nina Ennismore had experience breeding Rex rabbits and recognized that Kallibunker's coat was unusual. After consulting her veterinarian and geneticist A. C. Jude, she was advised to breed Kallibunker back to his mother. This produced three kittens, including two curly-coated males. One later died, while the surviving male, Poldhu, and Kallibunker were used to establish further Cornish Rex lines.

During the 1950s and 1960s, the breed had a very small gene pool and required careful breeding to establish a healthy foundation. Cornish Rex cats were crossed with domestic cats and several established breeds, including Siamese, Burmese, Russian Blues, American Shorthairs and British Shorthairs. These breeding programs helped increase genetic diversity while preserving the distinctive curly coat and elegant body type.

In 1956, an article about the Cornish Rex was published in Life magazine, bringing considerable attention to the unusual breed. In 1957, Cornish Rex cats were imported to North America, where breeding programs helped establish the breed in the United States.

The Cornish Rex was accepted for CFA registration in 1962 and achieved CFA championship status in 1964. GCCF granted the breed official recognition with championship status in 1967. Today, the Cornish Rex is recognized for its extremely soft marcel-waved coat, long-legged athletic body, intelligence, affectionate personality and playful, kitten-like behavior.

Physical Characteristic

Important: The Cornish Rex may appear delicate because of its slender build, but its body is firm, muscular and surprisingly substantial for its size.
Traits Details
Size Small to medium-sized
Weight Females approximately 5 to 7 lb (2.3 to 3.2 kg); males approximately 8 to 10 lb (3.6 to 4.5 kg)
Body Slender, hard, muscular and athletic with a deep chest and naturally arched back
Head Small and egg-shaped with high, prominent cheekbones
Eyes Medium to large, oval-shaped and slightly upward-slanting
Ears Large, full at the base, erect and set high on the head
Legs Very long, slender and muscular
Feet Small and slightly oval with long, nimble toes
Tail Long, slender, extremely flexible and tapering toward the tip
Neck Long and slender
Common Colors All colors and patterns are recognized
Distinctive Feature Extremely soft marcel-waved coat, long legs, arched back, large ears and egg-shaped head

Coat & Grooming

Coat Type: Very short, fine, soft, silky and relatively dense.

Coat Pattern: Distinctive marcel waves that may be tight or loose and extend across the body.

Grooming Frequency: Minimal regular grooming is usually required because the coat does not normally need frequent brushing or combing.

Bathing: Occasional bathing may be necessary because some Cornish Rex cats can accumulate excess oils on the skin. The coat should be dried thoroughly after bathing.

Shedding: Minimal compared with many other cats.

Unique Feature: The coat lacks the normal guard hairs found in most cats and forms soft, distinctive waves close to the skin. The curls may also extend to the eyebrows and whiskers.

Longhair Variation: The standard Cornish Rex is a short-haired breed.

Health & Care Consideration

Common Health Concerns

Progressive Retinal Atrophy (PRA) - A genetic eye condition that causes gradual degeneration of the retina and can eventually result in vision loss or blindness. Genetic testing can help identify cats carrying certain forms of the condition.

Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM) - A heart condition involving abnormal thickening of the heart muscle. Veterinary cardiac examinations can help detect heart abnormalities and support appropriate monitoring.

Patellar Luxation - A condition in which the kneecap can move out of its normal position. It may cause discomfort, abnormal movement or difficulty walking in affected cats.

Skin Problems and Sunburn - The Cornish Rex has a very thin coat with no guard hairs, which provides less protection from sunlight and environmental conditions. Excessive sun exposure can increase the risk of skin irritation and sunburn.

Cold Sensitivity - The thin coat provides limited insulation, so Cornish Rex cats can become cold more easily than cats with thicker coats. Warm indoor resting areas are especially useful.

Care Considerations

Nutrition: Provide a complete and balanced diet appropriate for the cat's age, size, activity level and health requirements. Fresh, clean water should always be available.

Weight Management: Monitor food portions and body condition regularly. Cornish Rex cats can have large appetites despite their slender appearance, so controlled feeding can help prevent excessive weight gain.

Exercise & Enrichment: Provide daily interactive play, climbing opportunities, puzzle feeders, toys and other activities that encourage physical movement and mental stimulation. Their high activity level makes regular exercise particularly important.

Dental Care: Brush the teeth regularly using veterinarian-approved cat toothpaste and arrange professional dental care when recommended.

Nail Care: Trim the nails regularly to prevent overgrowth and provide suitable scratching posts or scratching surfaces.

Eye Care: Check the eyes regularly for unusual discharge, redness or irritation. Gently clean normal buildup with a soft, damp cloth when necessary. Persistent or abnormal eye changes should be examined by a veterinarian.

Ear Care: Check the ears regularly for wax buildup, redness, unusual odor or discharge. Cornish Rex cats may accumulate wax more readily and may require gentle ear cleaning when necessary.

Veterinary Care: Regular veterinary examinations are recommended for vaccinations, dental health, weight monitoring and early detection of disease. Cardiac and orthopedic concerns can also be discussed with a veterinarian when appropriate.

Genetic Testing: When obtaining a Cornish Rex kitten from a breeder, ask about available genetic testing and the health history of the parents, particularly for known inherited conditions such as Progressive Retinal Atrophy.

Important: Breed-associated health conditions are potential risks and do not mean that every Cornish Rex will develop them. Regular preventive veterinary care can help detect health problems early.
