Oriental Shorthair Cat

History & Origin

The Oriental Shorthair originated in England during the 1950s as breeders worked to develop cats with the elegant body type and personality of the Siamese but with a much wider range of coat colors and patterns. The breed belongs to the Siamese breed group and shares the same long, slender body structure, wedge-shaped head and highly social temperament.

After World War II, the number of breeding cats and established breeding programs in Britain had been greatly reduced. As breeders rebuilt their programs, Siamese cats were crossed with other breeds, including Russian Blues, British Shorthairs, Abyssinians and domestic cats. These crosses produced cats with the characteristic Siamese body type but without the traditional pointed coat pattern.

The non-pointed offspring were repeatedly bred back to Siamese cats. Because the pointed pattern of the Siamese is genetically recessive, these breeding programs could produce both pointed and non-pointed kittens. The non-pointed cats eventually became the foundation of the modern Oriental breed, while selected pointed offspring could be returned to Siamese breeding programs.

At first, different colors were treated as separate breeds. Examples included the Foreign White, Havana and Oriental Spotted Tabby. As breeders produced an increasingly large number of possible colors and patterns, it became impractical to maintain a separate breed for every color. The non-pointed cats were therefore grouped together as the Oriental Shorthair and Oriental Longhair.

Oriental Shorthairs were imported into the United States during the 1970s. American breeders continued developing the breed, including additional crosses between American Shorthairs and Siamese cats that introduced further colors and patterns. The breed became increasingly established as a colorful counterpart to the Siamese while retaining the same distinctive oriental body type.

The first Oriental breed club in the United States, Oriental Shorthairs International, helped organize breeders and promote recognition of the breed. CFA accepted Oriental Shorthairs for registration in 1974 and granted championship status in 1977. In 1988, Oriental Longhairs were also accepted by CFA, and the broader breed became known simply as the Oriental, with Shorthair and Longhair divisions.

Today, the Oriental Shorthair is recognized for its extremely wide range of colors and patterns, sleek short coat, long elegant body, large ears and almond-shaped eyes. It is also known for its highly social and talkative personality and its strong attachment to human companions.

Physical Characteristic

Important: The Oriental Shorthair has a slender appearance but should have firm, well-developed muscles. Its long lines and proportions should remain balanced rather than appearing excessively thin or fragile.

Eyes Almond-shaped, medium-sized and set at an angle following the lines of the wedge-shaped head

Neck Long and slender
Common Colors Hundreds of color and pattern combinations, including solids, tabbies, torties, torbies, silvers, smokes and particolors
Distinctive Feature Extremely large ears, almond-shaped eyes, long triangular head, slender athletic body and wide variety of coat colors and patterns

Coat & Grooming

Coat Type: Very short, fine, glossy and close-lying.

Coat Pattern: Available in a very wide range of solid, tabby, tortoiseshell, torbie, smoke, silver and particolor patterns.

Grooming Frequency: Minimal grooming is required. Weekly brushing can help remove loose hair and maintain the sleek appearance of the coat.

Bathing: Regular bathing is generally unnecessary unless the cat becomes unusually dirty or develops an oily or irritated coat. Use cat-safe grooming products when bathing is required.

Shedding: Low to moderate, with regular brushing helping to remove loose hairs.

Unique Feature: The short, sleek coat allows the breed's wide range of colors and patterns to appear clearly while emphasizing its long, elegant body.

Longhair Variation: The Oriental Longhair is the long-coated counterpart of the Oriental Shorthair and is treated as a separate coat division in some cat associations.

Health & Care Consideration

Common Health Concerns

Amyloidosis - A condition involving abnormal deposits of amyloid protein in organs and tissues. Oriental Shorthairs and related Siamese-group cats are particularly associated with hepatic amyloidosis, which can interfere with liver function and may become life-threatening.

Hereditary Retinal Degeneration - A genetic eye condition that can cause progressive deterioration of the retina. Early signs may include difficulty seeing in low light, followed by progressive vision loss. Genetic testing can help identify cats carrying known disease-associated mutations.

Pica - Some Oriental Shorthairs may develop pica, a behavior involving chewing or eating non-food materials. This can create risks of poisoning, intestinal blockage and other health problems, so inappropriate chewing or eating should be discussed with a veterinarian.

Anesthesia Sensitivity - Oriental Shorthairs may have increased sensitivity to anesthesia. The veterinarian should be informed of the breed before surgical procedures, including routine operations and dental procedures.

Dental Disease - Like other cats, Oriental Shorthairs can develop dental disease. Regular tooth brushing and veterinary dental examinations can help maintain good oral health.

Care Considerations

Nutrition: Provide a complete and balanced diet appropriate for the cat's age, size, activity level and health requirements. Fresh, clean water should always be available.

Weight Management: Monitor food portions and body condition regularly. Oriental Shorthairs have naturally slender bodies, but free-feeding can still contribute to excessive weight gain. Several controlled meals throughout the day can be useful.

Exercise & Enrichment: Provide daily interactive play, puzzle feeders, cat trees, climbing opportunities, toys and training activities. Oriental Shorthairs are highly intelligent and active and need regular mental and physical stimulation.

Dental Care: Brush the teeth regularly using veterinarian-approved cat toothpaste and arrange professional dental care when recommended.

Nail Care: Trim the nails regularly to prevent overgrowth and provide suitable scratching posts or scratching surfaces.

Eye Care: The eyes generally do not require special treatment, but they should be checked regularly for unusual discharge, redness or irritation. Signs of vision problems, such as bumping into objects, should be evaluated by a veterinarian.

Ear Care: The large ears generally do not require special routine care, but they should be checked regularly for abnormal discharge, dark debris, unusual odor or other signs of infection or ear mites.

Veterinary Care: Regular veterinary examinations are recommended for vaccinations, dental health, weight monitoring and early detection of disease. The veterinarian should also be informed of the breed's potential anesthesia sensitivity before surgical or dental procedures.

Genetic Testing: When obtaining an Oriental Shorthair kitten from a breeder, ask about available genetic testing and the health history of the parents. Testing may be particularly relevant for hereditary retinal degeneration and other known inherited conditions.

Important: Breed-associated health conditions are potential risks and do not mean that every Oriental Shorthair will develop them. Responsible breeding, appropriate genetic testing and regular veterinary care can help reduce and manage these risks.
