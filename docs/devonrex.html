Devon Rex Cat

History & Origin

The Devon Rex originated in Buckfastleigh, Devon, England, during the late 1950s and early 1960s. The breed developed from a naturally occurring genetic mutation that produced a distinctive short, curly coat. The Devon Rex is an independent breed from the Cornish Rex, despite the similarities in their curly coats.

In 1959, a curly-coated male kitten was born in a garden near an abandoned tin mine in Buckfastleigh. A feral tomcat with a curly coat was known to live around the mine, and it is believed that he was responsible for passing the unusual coat characteristic to the kitten's mother. The kitten was a brownish-black male with a curly coat and was adopted by Beryl Cox, who named him Kirlee.

Kirlee was initially believed to carry the same mutation responsible for the Cornish Rex. He was therefore introduced into breeding programs associated with the developing Rex cats. Test breeding eventually demonstrated that his coat mutation was genetically different from the mutation responsible for the Cornish Rex. The Devon Rex therefore developed as a separate breed rather than as another line of Cornish Rex.

Kirlee had several characteristics that distinguished him from Cornish Rex cats. His coat formed a looser, more irregular wave, while his whiskers and eyebrows were short and often broken or crinkled. He also had very large, low-set ears, large expressive eyes, high cheekbones and a short muzzle, giving him the distinctive elfin or pixie-like appearance associated with the Devon Rex today.

Because the early Devon Rex population descended from a very small genetic foundation, breeders had to carefully expand the gene pool. Outcrossing programs were introduced using other breeds, including American Shorthairs and British Shorthairs in CFA breeding programs. These efforts helped increase genetic diversity while preserving the Devon Rex's characteristic coat, body structure and facial appearance.

The breed gradually gained recognition from cat associations. ACFA recognized the Devon Rex as a separate breed in 1972, while CFA granted separate registration in 1979, provisional status in 1981 and championship status in 1983. The breed became established internationally as a distinct Rex breed with its own genetic mutation and breed standard.

Today, the Devon Rex is recognized for its unusual pixie-like appearance, soft wavy coat, large low-set ears, prominent cheekbones and large eyes. Its personality is equally distinctive, with high intelligence, curiosity, mischievous behavior and a strong desire to remain close to people.

Physical Characteristic

Important: The Devon Rex has a distinctive pixie-like appearance created by its large low-set ears, prominent cheekbones, large eyes and short muzzle. Despite its elfin appearance, the body is muscular and surprisingly substantial for its size.

Size Small to medium-sized
Weight Approximately 6 to 9 lb (2.7 to 4.1 kg); males may be somewhat larger
Body Muscular, compact and supple with a broad chest and sturdy build
Head Short, broad modified wedge-shaped head with prominent cheekbones, strong chin and short muzzle
Eyes Large, oval-shaped and expressive, set at a slight angle
Ears Very large, wide at the base and set low on the sides of the head
Legs Long and slender, with the hind legs longer than the forelegs
Feet Small and oval with long toes
Tail Long, slender and tapering toward the tip
Neck Slender and relatively long
Common Colors All colors and patterns are recognized
Distinctive Feature Very large low-set ears, large eyes, prominent cheekbones, short muzzle and soft wavy coat

Coat & Grooming

Coat Type: Very short, soft, fine and velvety with sparse, short rexed guard hairs.

Coat Pattern: Wavy or curly throughout the body, with the ideal coat showing an even, full wave.

Grooming Frequency: Minimal grooming is generally required. Gentle handling is recommended because the delicate coat can be easily disturbed or broken.

Bathing: Occasional bathing may be necessary because the sparse coat can allow oils to build up on the skin. Use a cat-safe shampoo and avoid excessive bathing.

Shedding: Low shedding, although Devon Rex cats do shed and should not be considered completely non-shedding.

Unique Feature: The coat is made up of short, soft hairs with a distinctive wave or ripple. The eyebrows and whiskers are also short and often crinkled.

Longhair Variation: The Devon Rex is a short-haired breed.

Health & Care Consideration

Common Health Concerns

Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM) - A heart condition involving abnormal thickening of the heart muscle. HCM may initially show few obvious signs, so appropriate veterinary monitoring and cardiac screening can help with early detection.

Polycystic Kidney Disease (PKD) - A genetic condition in which cysts develop in the kidneys and can progressively interfere with kidney function. Cats showing increased thirst, frequent urination, reduced appetite or unexplained weight loss should be evaluated by a veterinarian.

Congenital Myasthenic Syndrome (CMS) - A genetic neuromuscular condition that can cause muscle weakness. Genetic testing is available and can be useful in breeding programs to identify carriers.

Patellar Luxation - A condition in which the kneecap moves out of its normal position. It may cause abnormal movement, discomfort or difficulty walking in affected cats.

Blood Type Incompatibility - Devon Rex populations have a relatively high frequency of blood type B. In breeding, incompatible blood types can cause serious problems in newborn kittens, including neonatal isoerythrolysis. Blood typing is therefore important for breeding cats.

Care Considerations

Nutrition: Provide a complete and balanced diet appropriate for the cat's age, size, activity level and health requirements. Fresh, clean water should always be available.

Weight Management: Monitor food portions and body condition regularly. Some Devon Rex cats have strong appetites and may require controlled feeding to prevent excessive weight gain.

Exercise & Enrichment: Provide daily interactive play, climbing structures, puzzle toys, scratching posts and other activities that encourage physical movement and mental stimulation. Devon Rex cats are highly active and intelligent and can become bored when they lack sufficient interaction.

Dental Care: Brush the teeth regularly using veterinarian-approved cat toothpaste and arrange professional dental care when recommended.

Nail Care: Trim the nails regularly to prevent overgrowth and provide suitable scratching posts or scratching surfaces.

Eye Care: Check the eyes regularly to ensure they remain clear and bright. Gently clean normal buildup when necessary. Unusual discharge, redness or persistent irritation should be examined by a veterinarian.

Ear Care: Check the large ears regularly for wax buildup, redness, unusual odor or discharge. Clean the ears gently when necessary and seek veterinary attention if discomfort or abnormal discharge occurs.

Veterinary Care: Regular veterinary examinations are recommended for vaccinations, dental health, weight monitoring and early detection of disease. Cardiac and orthopedic screening can also be discussed with a veterinarian when appropriate.

Genetic Testing: When obtaining a Devon Rex kitten from a breeder, ask about available genetic testing and the health history of the parents. Testing can be particularly useful for conditions such as congenital myasthenic syndrome, while appropriate screening can also help reduce inherited health risks.

Important: Breed-associated health conditions are potential risks and do not mean that every Devon Rex will develop them. Responsible breeding, appropriate genetic testing and regular veterinary care can help reduce and manage these risks.
