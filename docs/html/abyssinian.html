Abyssinian Cat

History & Origin

The Abyssinian is considered one of the oldest established cat breeds, but its exact origin remains uncertain. Despite the breed's name, modern genetic research does not support the idea that Abyssinians originated specifically in ancient Abyssinia, the former name for Ethiopia. GCCF states that DNA analysis points to an origin near the coast of the Indian Ocean, while CFA notes genetic connections to coastal areas of the Indian Ocean and Southeast Asia. The breed's resemblance to cats shown in ancient Egyptian art has contributed to the long-standing belief that Abyssinians descended directly from ancient Egyptian cats, but this has not been established as fact.

The name Abyssinian became associated with the breed because some of the earliest cats exhibited in England were imported from Ethiopia. One famous early Abyssinian was Zula, who was exhibited at the Crystal Palace cat show in London in 1871. The appearance of these cats helped establish the breed in the British cat fancy, although records from this period are incomplete and the exact ancestry of the early cats is difficult to reconstruct.

During the late 19th and early 20th centuries, British breeders developed the Abyssinian into a more consistent pedigree breed. GCCF records references to the breed dating to the 1890s and states that the Abyssinian was officially recognized by GCCF in 1929. The breed's characteristic ticked coat, elegant body, wedge-shaped head, and alert appearance became important parts of its established type.

Abyssinians were later introduced to the United States in the early 1900s. CFA states that they were among the original breeds registered by the organization in 1906. Over time, breeding programs in the United States and other countries further established the Abyssinian as a recognized international pedigree breed.

Important Note: The commonly repeated story that modern Abyssinians are direct descendants of cats from ancient Egypt has not been established as fact. Their resemblance to cats depicted in ancient Egyptian art is documented, but their exact historical ancestry remains uncertain.

Physical Characteristic

Traits Details
Size Medium-sized, with males generally larger than females
Weight Adult females: 6 to 9 lb (2.7 to 4.1 kg); adult males: 7 to 12 lb (3.2 to 5.4 kg)
Body Lithe, muscular, graceful, and well-balanced
Head Modified, slightly rounded wedge shape with gentle contours
Eyes Large, expressive, almond-shaped eyes
Ears Large, alert, moderately pointed, broad and cupped at the base
Legs Slim, fine-boned, long, and well-muscled
Feet Small, oval, and compact; give the appearance of standing on tiptoe
Tail Fairly long, thick at the base, and tapering
Neck Gracefully arched
Common Colors Ruddy, cinnamon, blue, and fawn; silver varieties are also recognized by TICA

Coat & Grooming

Coat Type: Short, fine, soft, dense, and resilient with a lustrous sheen.

Coat Pattern: Ticked coat in which individual hairs have multiple alternating bands of dark and light color. The darker color is concentrated toward the tips of the hairs.

Grooming Frequency: Low maintenance. Brushing or combing a few times per month is generally sufficient to control shedding and remove dead hair.

Bathing: Generally unnecessary for routine care unless needed for a specific reason or for showing.

Shedding: Relatively low compared with many longer-haired breeds.

Unique Feature: The individual bands of color on the hairs create the Abyssinian's characteristic shimmering appearance.

Characteristic & Temperament

Health & Care Consideration

Common Health Concerns

Pyruvate Kinase Deficiency (PK-Def)- An inherited disorder that affects red blood cells and can cause anemia. Genetic testing is available.

Progressive Retinal Atrophy (PRA) - An inherited retinal disease that progressively damages the retina and can eventually result in blindness. Genetic testing is available for certain forms.

Patellar Luxation (PL) - A condition in which the kneecap can move out of its normal position.

Renal Amyloidosis (RA) - A condition involving the accumulation of amyloid material in the kidneys and potentially other organs. TICA notes that there is currently no DNA/genetic test for renal amyloidosis.

Care Considerations

Nutrion: Provide fresh, clean water and a complete, balanced diet appropriate for the cat's life stage and individual needs.

Exercise % Enrichment: Provide daily opportunities for physical activity and mental stimulation. Cat trees, elevated perches, interactive toys, and play sessions are appropriate for the breed's high activity level.

Dental Care: Regular dental care can help reduce the risk of dental disease.

Nail Care: Nails should be trimmed regularly; TICA recommends approximately every two weeks.

Eye Care: Check the eyes regularly for unusual discharge, redness, cloudiness, or irritation. Abyssinians can be susceptible to Progressive Retinal Atrophy (PRA), an inherited eye condition that can eventually cause blindness, so appropriate genetic testing is important when obtaining a kitten from a breeder.

Ear Care: Check the large ears regularly for wax, dirt, redness, or unusual discharge. Clean gently when necessary using a veterinarian-recommended ear cleaner, and consult a veterinarian if irritation or discharge is present.

Veterinary Care: Regular veterinary examinations are recommended for preventive care and monitoring.

Genetic Testing: When obtaining an Abyssinian from a breeder, ask about genetic testing for conditions such as PK-Def and PRA and request documentation of test results where available.

Important: Breed-associated health conditions are potential risks and do not mean that every Abyssinian will develop them.
